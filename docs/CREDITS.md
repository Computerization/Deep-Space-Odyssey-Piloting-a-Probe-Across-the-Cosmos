## Ocean reflection refinement

The ocean shader uses an isotropic Gaussian distribution of wave slopes, with Cox–Munk mean-square slope `0.003 + 0.00512 U`. The assumed 5 m/s wind is a representative rendering parameter, not observed weather at the historical encounter. The Fresnel term and shadowing remain simplified. Reference: Su et al., *Observations of reflectance distribution around sunglint from a coastal ocean platform* (2002), NASA-hosted paper: https://science-data.larc.nasa.gov/CRAVE/papers/SuApplOpt02.pdf.

## Earth radiance convention (0.34)

Earth's diffuse surface uses the Lambertian cosine response with radiance expressed in solar-irradiance / pi units. The normalized water microfacet response and normalized atmospheric phase functions therefore receive a factor of pi in those same units. This replaces the earlier unrelated water and atmospheric multipliers and removes the warm glint tint. The 5 m/s isotropic ocean roughness, approximate masking, RGB scattering coefficients and reconstructed clouds remain approximations. Atmospheric transmission preserves separate RGB channels. Incoming sunlight at the ground and ocean glint uses the same precomputed density columns and RGB extinction as clouds and view transport (0.44). This corrects omitted direct-sun attenuation; it does not add diffuse sky illumination or multiple scattering. Revision 0.52 adds the limited daytime cloud-sky contribution described below. The source colour composite is not a measured spectral reflectance field. Principles: https://www.pbr-book.org/3ed-2018/Reflection_Models/Lambertian_Reflection — https://ebruneton.github.io/precomputed_atmospheric_scattering/atmosphere/functions.glsl

## Cloud view integration (0.35)

View-segment optical depth integrates the vertical profile analytically; its first moment supplies a density-weighted sunlight sample. Bounded lateral sampling responds to the weather texture's resolved footprint. The underlying transmittance follows the exponential of integrated extinction and composes multiplicatively across successive segments. At resolved, near-Earth grazing views, the two existing weather reads supply separate near/far column integrals and a combined density centroid (0.70). Camera distance, magnification and grazing angle smoothly return to the original sampling elsewhere. This reduces sampled-model artifacts without adding measured cloud detail. This remains a limited single-scattering reconstruction with an approximate ambient term. Reference principles: https://pbr-book.org/4ed/Volume_Scattering/Transmittance

## Reconstructed cloud height (0.79)

Coverage and vertical optical depth retain the source map's fine detail. At loading, a 4 × 4 area average and bilinear reconstruction supply a regional height proxy, separating narrow brightness variations from the inferred 2.7–9 km top. Fine coverage (R) and reconstructed height (G) are packed in one RG8 texture; mip filtering and every ray lookup use this shared field. The existing 1.4 km base and normalized vertical profile remain. View rays, cloud sunlight and ground shadows share this sampler, including the near-view two-column path. The height field remains an inference from the credited visual map. [NASA's MODIS cloud product](https://modis.gsfc.nasa.gov/data/dataprod/mod06.php) retrieves cloud-top properties from infrared measurements and optical properties from visible/near-infrared channels; the visual map alone does not supply measured cloud heights. Source resolution and incomplete multiple scattering remain limits on close-view realism.

## Atmospheric composition

Radiance is composed as in-scattered light plus background radiance multiplied by RGB transmittance. The renderer uses a linear multiplicative pass followed by scattering. Cloud radiance includes air only up to its opacity-weighted depth; opaque clouds occlude the clear-air contribution behind them. This representative-depth approximation avoids a second atmosphere march at every cloud sample. It is not fully coupled cloud/air multiple scattering. See the [atmospheric transport reference](https://ebruneton.github.io/precomputed_atmospheric_scattering/atmosphere/functions.glsl) and [Three.js blend factors](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/Material.html).

## Ground cloud-shadow consistency (0.47)

The surface integrates the same reconstructed cloud field along the sunlight ray used by the volume model. This replaces a separate flat UV displacement and fixed minimum solar elevation. Source imagery and inferred cloud heights remain unchanged; visible-image brightness alone is not a cloud-height measurement. Ground shading uses bounded sampling, and air/cloud multiple scattering remains approximate. Qualitative orbital reference: [NASA/Don Pettit, Earth's Day-Night Shadow](https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/31347/). The [NASA ISS cloud-and-shadow discussion](https://science.nasa.gov/earth/earth-observatory/crepuscular-rays-and-cumulonimbus-clouds-153537/) and [NASA ISCCP cloud-type definitions](https://isccp.giss.nasa.gov/cloudtypes.html) distinguish cloud structure and optical thickness. Reference photographs are used for review and are not added to the game.

## Daytime cloud sky illumination (0.52)

Clouds receive an additional single-Rayleigh-scattered sky term, using the existing molecular/aerosol columns and coefficients. The analytic plane-parallel transport integral is averaged exactly in azimuth and integrated over six Gaussian elevations. The result is irradiance relative to solar flux. It is evaluated at the existing representative cloud depth; an accumulated vertical-depth approximation attenuates light within clouds. The previous direct illumination, imagery, exposure and air in front of clouds remain in place. This is a bounded approximation: it omits curved twilight, diffuse ground lighting, cloud/air feedback and higher scattering orders, and does not establish photographic equivalence. Numerical checks quantify quadrature error within this model. The general radiance-to-irradiance principle is described in [Bruneton's atmospheric transport reference](https://ebruneton.github.io/precomputed_atmospheric_scattering/atmosphere/functions.glsl). [NASA's EPIC Blue Marble gallery](https://epic.gsfc.nasa.gov/galleries/2022/blue_marble) provides real observational context; camera response, illumination and geography differ from this reconstructed scene. No reference image is shipped as a new asset.


## Reference rotation in local studies

Earth uses a 23.9345-hour sidereal period; Jupiter uses 9.9250 hours in System III (1965.0); Saturn uses the 10.656-hour Saturnian System III reference. Source: NASA NSSDCA [Earth](https://nssdc.gsfc.nasa.gov/planetary/factsheet/earthfact.html), [Jupiter](https://nssdc.gsfc.nasa.gov/planetary/factsheet/jupiterfact.html) and [Saturn](https://nssdc.gsfc.nasa.gov/planetary/factsheet/saturnfact.html) fact sheets, retrieved 15 November 2026. These reference frames are not measurements of every cloud's wind speed.

Elapsed encounter seconds shift the existing surface/cloud maps together in the prograde direction. Initial map longitudes remain authored; the source images retain their credited mixed dates. No differential winds, changing weather or historically calibrated prime-meridian reconstruction are added. Local study body centres and moon orientations retain their fixed historical snapshots. The axisymmetric ring profile is unchanged. Pausing, reviewing, photographing and restoring a visit use the same recorded encounter time.

The ring-shadow reconstruction (0.73) precomputes transmission from the existing radial optical-depth profile at the encounter’s fixed solar elevation. Filtering that transmitted light preserves transparent gaps before averaging. A padded 8,192-sample R16F texture is reused across epochs; 2 × 2 pixel quadrature and nine filtered solar samples replace the former 3 × 3 × 5 point samples. The shadow profile now extends to the visible ring geometry’s 2.323-equatorial-radius boundary, retaining both halves of the reconstructed F ring. The finite-Sun kernel remains a Gaussian approximation, and the source opacity and historical geometry retain their documented limitations. This changes reconstruction of the supplied data rather than adding measured ring structure.

The direct F-ring reconstruction (0.74) retains the same radius, Gaussian optical-depth profile, colour and physical mesh. At unresolved footprints, sixteen profile-centred samples integrate the scattered radiance and opacity with the square pixel’s linearized radial coverage. Resolved views retain the ordinary pixel samples, joined by a smooth transition. This reduces spatial undersampling; it adds no measured ring detail. Finite mesh coverage and grazing-angle linearization remain approximations.

## Historical surface encoding (0.79–0.97; Earth restored in 0.99)

Earth uses a 16K map encoded with Basis Universal ETC1S at quality 255; Moon now uses a 16K map at quality 180. Both use effort 2 and a complete sRGB-aware mip chain. Earth keeps its supported GPU format. The opaque lunar map prefers four-bit BC1 or ETC2 where supported: approximately 85.3 MiB at 16K or 21.3 MiB at 8K, including mips. Devices without those formats retain the loader fallback. The former 8K Moon BC7 path occupied about 42.7 MiB. The new Moon file is 18,847,147 bytes, up from 5,758,565 bytes; this is a deliberate source-detail tradeoff. On an 8K-limited device the compressed mip slightly increases measured source error against the former native 8K map; it is a compatibility path with lower texture storage, not a detail upgrade. Firefox retains its existing Earth fallback at an 8K texture limit. The original AVIF files remain offline fallbacks. Cloud RG8 adds approximately 42.7 MiB compared with its former R8 field. This explicitly trades a larger offline download for real image detail while bounding surface-map GPU memory. Reproduction: `build-surface-detail.py`; source dimensions, hashes and encoder arguments: `surface-detail-source.json`.

## Cloud source detail (0.90)

The 16384 × 8192 coverage field uses level-3 tiles from Toni Sagristà's [Gaia Sky NASA cloud atlas](https://repository.gaiasky.space/vt/vt-earth-clouds-nasa/000_20230125/index.html), whose metadata attributes NASA Visible Earth, Blue Marble: Clouds. NASA's [Blue Marble description](https://science.nasa.gov/earth/earth-observatory/the-blue-marble-true-color-global-imagery-at-1km-resolution/) credits Reto Stöckli (NASA GSFC) and Robert Simmon, and describes a composite assembled from visible and infrared imagery. Atlas pixel dimensions do not establish the observational resolution.

The atlas supplies coverage through absolute latitude 50°. A smooth 50–60° transition retains the existing [Solar System Scope / INOVE](https://www.solarsystemscope.com/textures/) polar composite beyond 60°, under [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/). Polar detail therefore remains limited by that 8K source. Processing combines aligned source imagery without displacement or sharpening, and encodes scalar coverage as full-range monochrome AVIF (libavif 1.4.2 / AOM, quality 65, speed 4). A separate 2048 × 1024 lossless scalar field stores regional mean brightness for the existing inferred-height model. Neither brightness nor inferred height is a measured three-dimensional weather field; the imagery does not reconstruct weather in 1977.

Development reproduction is in `build-cloud-detail.py`, with source and delivery hashes in `cloud-detail-source.json`. The two maps total 13,826,371 bytes. Their complete R8 mip chains occupy about 173.3 MiB at full resolution, approximately 88 MiB more than the former 8K RG8 field. Constrained texture devices resize coverage through the existing loader while retaining the regional grid. No atlas archive or raw reference is included in the game.

## Local cloud imagery (0.91)

The current near-view coverage uses the same credited Gaia Sky / NASA atlas at its native level-4 grid (32768 × 16384). Ninety-six 2048-pixel interiors, each encoded with an eight-pixel neighbour border, cover the non-polar band and the established 50–60° transition. The previous INOVE polar source and the independent 2K inferred-height field are retained. Grid dimensions describe the stored imagery, not verified observational resolution. No procedural detail or sharpened terrain is added.

Full-range monochrome AVIF uses AOM quality 55 for the tiles and quality 70 for the 8192 × 4096 global base. The base and tiles total 25,076,399 bytes; the unchanged height map adds 1,377,699 bytes. The source builder is `build-cloud-tiles.py`; current delivery hashes, sources, encoding and limits are recorded in `cloud-detail-source.json` in the development workspace. The full source atlas and lossless references remain outside the game.

### Gas-giant cloud reconstruction (0.103)

Jupiter and Saturn retain their credited global maps. An upper cloud deck models extinction, view depth and self-shadow within 90 km of the reference cloud level; height is inferred from map brightness. The former deterministic fine-noise field is removed; source imagery supplies cloud structure. Diffuse and single-scattering reflection terms replace the previous solid-like angular response. Differential zonal motion uses a model envelope of 120 m/s in local presentation time. These heights, scattering and winds are illustrative approximations, not measured weather or additional spacecraft image resolution. Saturn retains ring shadow, ringshine and its separately sampled polar imagery.

## Encounter and lighting revision (0.105)

The Great Red Spot is registered at approximately (0.792, 0.378) in the existing Voyager map’s bottom-origin UV coordinates. The map’s authored rotation phase now presents it on the illuminated side; this is not a retrieved 1979 System III orientation. Its roughly 23,000 km Voyager-era width follows [NASA’s Hubble account](https://science.nasa.gov/missions/hubble/nasas-hubble-shows-jupiters-great-red-spot-is-smaller-than-ever-measured/). The [polar jet interpretation](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/cassini/science/saturn/hexagon-in-motion/) remains distinct from the modern Cassini imagery used to show it. Historical sunlight leaves part of that cap in shadow.

Earth cloud lighting retains the original source coverage, height reconstruction, self-shadow and image sampling. Two increasingly diffuse terms extend the existing single multiple-scattering approximation. The coefficients are illustrative and bounded, not a measured reflectance retrieval. The [Apollo 17 image](https://www.nasa.gov/image-article/blue-marble-image-of-earth-from-apollo-17/) was consulted for qualitative cloud contrast; its weather, viewpoint, exposure and film response differ. Giant-planet reflection uses an illustrative normalized semi-infinite H-function approximation with effective single-scattering albedo 0.95 and a diffuse contribution. It replaces the over-bright oblique single-scattering response in both the mapped atmosphere and upper cloud deck. [Jupiter limb observations](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/search.jsp?R=19760045139) motivate checking the angular response, but do not calibrate these chosen coefficients. This is not a complete spectral aerosol model.

Titan’s pole uses the J2000 right ascension and declination in [JPL/NAIF pck00011](https://naif.jpl.nasa.gov/pub/naif/generic_kernels/pck/pck00011.tpc). A shared 350 km detached-layer centre replaces the previous 500 km Cassini-like value in both the optical-depth table and visible ray integration. [West et al. (2018)](https://arxiv.org/html/1804.10842v1) report Voyager-era heights of 300–350 km and later seasonal variation. Smooth northern dimming and a polar hood follow the qualitative appearance in [Voyager’s Titan image](https://science.nasa.gov/photojournal/titans-brighter-southern-hemisphere/); contrast and optical properties remain approximations. No new reference images are included in the game.

## Scale and opening (0.106)

Arrival cameras frame horizons while retaining the 58° lens, physical body dimensions and historical positions. Assisted cruise grows sublinearly with body radius, so traversing a giant takes longer than traversing a small world. The displayed diameter is equatorial; altitude is radial height above the oblate reference surface or cloud level, not a measured terrain clearance. The opening atmospheric explanation uses [NASA’s atmospheric-layer account](https://science.nasa.gov/earth/earth-atmosphere/earths-atmosphere-a-multi-layered-cake/) for the roughly 12 km mean troposphere and distinguishes it from the full atmosphere. No new imagery or runtime dependency is added.
